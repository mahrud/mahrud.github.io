## Or how to break the laws of mathematics and fix our society

### Abstract

From Pythagoras and Euclid to Einstein and Gödel, nothing is less mathematical than maths teachers claiming that their instructions are set in stone. The laws of mathematics are like any other law: they are meant to be broken, and this talk is about just that.

### Description

It is said that Pythagoras, the ancient Greek polymath famous for his theorem about right triangles, founded a cult in 6th century BC in which it was believed that every number was rational, meaning that it could be expressed as a ratio a/b of integers, and that they threw Hippasus overboard at sea for discovering that &radic;2 is in fact an irrational number. Few centuries later, Euclid wrote the rules of geometry based on five postulates, but it took nearly two millennia for mathematicians to finally discover that the breaking the fifth one, known as the parallel postulate, gives rise to logically consistent geometries such as spherical geometry and hyperbolic geometry, but this talk is not _just_ about history.

This talk is about breaking laws of modern mathematics, from pure fields like algebraic geometry to its applications in cryptography, biometric surveillance, and predictive policing. We all know how these algorithms, or "Weapons of Math Destruction" as Cathy O'Neil calls them, surround us and surveil or censor our actions, not only on the internet, but in every corner of our lives. We also know how AI has powered an exponential growth in this trend, bringing dystopian, tech-driven horrors to the front-lines where they are used to commit human rights abuses with precision. The UN calls these Lethal Autonomous Weapon Systems -- LAWS. I will explain the mathematics behind LAWS, and how our lives depend on illegal _mathematical_ instructions.

As it turns out, you don't need to be a mathematician to commit illegal acts of mathematics. I will introduce you to a few of my favorite open source mathematical projects and how even without formal mathematical training you can use your programming and hacking skills to commit positive change in society through mathematics. As a concrete example, I will tell you about the ongoing fight against undemocratic tactics like "political gerrymandering" in the United States.

I will also tell you about the ways in which mathematics is broken, for instance the academic publishing industry and university power-grabs that suppress dissent and protest among students and professors. Movements such as the Just Mathematics Collective, which began during the 2020 Black Lives Matter rebellion "to shift the global mathematics community towards justice, via genuine anti-racism, anti-militarism, and solidarity," are the front-lines of this effort, and we need help from both mathematicians and non-mathematicians to bring ethics back into mathematics for our own sake.

### Notes for organiers

While I'm a full-time mathematician employed by the Max Planck Institut für Mathematik in den Naturwissenschaften, I'm a hacker at heart and have previously given talks and presentations about math, cryptography, or surveillance to both technical and non-technical audiences. For instance at the Crypto & Privacy Village at DEF CON 26 I talked about kick-starting a collaboration with the Tor Project, Mozilla, and Cloudflare to deploy Cloudflare's first onion service edge server in order to improve privacy and security for Tor users.
