What the society considers as a violent crime is critical in determining the legal, ethical, and ultimately moral response to stop and compensate? the violence done. In a retrubituive? justice system, when a violent crime is committed by an individual, our social contract provides the legal guidelines towards punishing the offender. But what happens when it is not possible to identify an individual offender? What compensation does the social contract provide when (in)action by the people and their government causes lead poisoning in a child or a heatstroke in an elder? An individual who shouts "FIRE" in a crowded theater is prosecuted, even if no single person is hurt by it, but how do we respond when a large portion of a community contributes to injury to an individual or minority within the commonity?

Since 2001, with passage of the Patriot Act mass surveillance by our government secretly targeted many groups within our community. What started with surveillance of muslims and people of middle eastern ancestry by wire taps? and undercover agents in mosques soon expanded to police cameras everywhere and massive NSA phone surveillance of everyone, violating our right to privacy and fourth amendment protections against illegal search. While the pot of illegal spying programs warmed more and more, we were the frogs blissfully giving consent to relinquishing more and more of our privacy. This is violence under the cover of law and secret CISA judges which we continue to permit in the name of security. [Quote about giving up liberty/privacy for security and getting neither]

With the rise of online advertisement and tracking, this violence shifted from the government to Silicon Valley corporations, some famous like Facebook and Google, and some in the shadows like data brokers(name names). The corporations collected and traded our location, habits, and preferences to build massive databases, which they used to manipulate us for profit and political gain (Trump's Cambridge Analytica). The convenience of "the Algorithm" providing an unending stream of content lulled us into consenting to violence without realizing what exactly we consented to. The amount of data collected by these corporations _each year_ is enough to fill many data centers.

And now we live in the midst of the next stage, when Amazon doorbell cameras provide recordings of unconsenting by passers to the police, data brokers like BLAH sell data to IRS/DHS?, Clearview AI sells facial recognition, backed by every photo of a loved one you ever uploaded to the internet, to police departments and leading to wrongful arrests, and Surveillance as a Service companies like Flock Safety sell access to their nationwide network of vehicle-tracking cameras to masked ICE agents searching for legal immigrants, dystopian authorities searching for someone who self-administered an abortion, and most evil of them all, homeowner associations! (weird joke?) This is violence.

But who is the violent offender here? The Homeowner's Association for purchasing the data? Clearview AI for brokering the data and algorithms refining them? Amazon for selling access to doorbell cameras? The thousands of individuals buying and installing those surveillance cameras, or elected mayors and city councils who signed contracts with Flock Safety to surveil us? Where do we draw the line of violence, and more importantly, how can we stop legal violence committed by the masses?

We live in a large, unwieldy house party where everyone--the host, guests, the landlord, the neighbour across, the local police department and city hall, and even federal drones above--is constantly monitoring, recording, and violating our privacy and ultimately our civil liberties.

In a lawless society, one wouldn't be at fault for assuming the moral and ethical response to violence may include violence, but what is one to do if the violence is being perpetuated at every corner by other well-intentioned members of the society? And what about our society then, where the laws provide cover for the perpetuators of this violence? For those of us who opted for physical webcam covers in the 2010's, are we at fault for ripping off or spray painting the surveillance cameras and doorbell cameras in our neighbourhoods?

The American Bill of Rights is too outdated for the kinds of violence that did not exist three hundred years ago (but still has room for the amendment baning army from coming to your house!). We need a new moral, ethical, and legal framework that protects our privacy and liberty as intertwined rights, not as a trade-off force upon us by government agencies, tech corporations, and, yes, even the pesky homeowner's associations!

If this matters to you, consider signing [this petition](https://petition.postme.mn/) or joining a [community feedback session](https://postme.mn/index.php/community-feedback-session/) via Zoom tonight (1/26) at 6pm CST. The petition and session are endorsed by groups such as ACLU and EFF, and RT4MN.

If you'd like more background on this issue, the NYTimes article on [Clearview AI](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/01/18/technology/clearview-privacy-facial-recognition.html) and [its operation](https://postme.mn/index.php/clearview-ai/) in Hennepin County and after the [Capitol attack](https://www.nytimes.com/2021/01/09/technology/facial-recognition-clearview-capitol.html) are good places to start, as well as [this article](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/12/29/technology/facial-recognition-misidentify-jail.html) on the third known Black man to be wrongfully arrested based on FR. For a more technical perspective, this is a good [report by the EFF](https://www.eff.org/pages/face-recognition).

Lastly, please consider forwarding this email to others in the Minneapolis/Saint Paul area who may share similar concerns. Facial recognition is one of the few areas where mathematicians have had a hand in social injustices, and I hope we can have a role in fixing those injustices as well.
