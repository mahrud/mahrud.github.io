Akin to Claire Saffitz' Gourmet Makes from BA videos.


I have a terribly written draft about this that maybe I'll finish some day, but here are some of my favorites:
- a lot of the skills that an advanced degree teaches you are about self-realization (learning your limits) and self-actualization (reaching your limits/potential), which I think are important in personal growth, even beyond just a career.
- on the more practical side, being a mathematician carries the understanding that you have certain skills that make your argument on any matter be heard. A great example of this for me is Moon Duchin's work on gerrymandering. She's a geometric group theorist, but the society values skills like perseverance and critical thinking to the point that the she can get in front of the PA supreme court and be considered an expert witness. (This one is sometimes confused with elitism. Elitism is a problem, but this is still true)
- I believe that in a just and peaceful world has a place, or even a need, for mathematicians, just as it has a place for authors, artists, or farmers. What brings me a sense of peace is that we're not just cogs on the gears of capitalism, so our roles here must be motivated by human nature and someone has to do it. This kinda reminds me of Tzadikim Nistarim in judaism, but I won't go into that. This doesn't mean this is the only role that we play, and I think it's important to not be completely occupied with math. 
- continuing off the last sentence, I think a just education is no less necessary for a just world than a fair justice system or healthcare system. I don't know if lack of diversity in math departments has a causal relationship with lack of wealth equity, but I doubt if one can be achieved without the other
